HOW IT WORKS

Face tracking, learned patterns, and supporting research

01 / A virtual mesh for face filters

Google’s MediaPipe Face Landmarker estimates 478 points in 3D across your face from camera images. We use those points to shape a virtual mesh and attach the filter to your cheeks, forehead or lower face. We chose it to help the filter follow your head as you turn, instead of sitting like a flat sticker on the screen.

Face Landmarker · Google MediaPipe

02 / How a pattern can fool detection

These papers study adversarial patches: patterns whose pixels are adjusted to make a detector less confident that it sees a face. Yang and colleagues found that some learned patches could even look like tiny faces and trigger extra boxes; their improved methods aim to suppress both the real face and those false detections.

Kaziakhmedov and colleagues printed black-and-white patterns and attached them to medical masks or directly to the face to test MTCNN. VIPatch combines a gradient-colored mask with a bandage sticker to target detectors that use both visible and infrared images.

In this demo, the cheeks and forehead give small areas for a pattern while leaving the eyes clear; the lower-face mask lets us compare broader coverage. These are experiment choices, not proven best spots. We demonstrate the idea digitally with Tiny Face Detector.

03 / Compare the same shape

Switch between a learned pattern and plain gray on exactly the same surface. A larger covering may work by occlusion alone. A remaining bounding box is a valid result; shape and fit do not guarantee avoidance.

This tests digital video, not printed masks or paint. Forehead hair and hands are not segmented, so the estimated surface can overlap them. Fit with your face unobstructed and test gentle turns.
